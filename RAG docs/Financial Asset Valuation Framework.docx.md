# Financial Asset Valuation Framework for an ML/DS Recommendation System

## Based on John Hull’s “Options, Futures and Other Derivatives (9th Edition)”

# Executive Summary

This document summarizes the financial valuation methodologies, mathematical models, risk measures, and quantitative finance concepts required to develop a Machine Learning and Data Science solution capable of recommending the most appropriate valuation method for different classes of financial assets.

The objective of the proposed system is not only to estimate asset values but also to determine which valuation methodology is theoretically most suitable given the characteristics of the asset, market conditions, available data, and risk factors.

The framework is primarily inspired by the quantitative finance models presented in John Hull’s “Options, Futures and Other Derivatives”, which is considered one of the most influential references in derivative pricing and financial engineering.

---

# 1\. Problem Definition

Financial assets are heterogeneous and require different valuation approaches.

Examples:

* A government bond is valued using discounted cash flows.

* A stock can be valued using dividend discount models or comparable companies.

* A futures contract follows cost-of-carry pricing.

* A European option is commonly priced using Black-Scholes.

* An American option is typically valued using binomial trees.

* Exotic derivatives often require Monte Carlo simulations.

* Credit products depend on default probabilities and recovery rates.

Selecting the wrong valuation method can produce inaccurate prices and lead to poor investment decisions.

The proposed ML/DS system aims to:

1. Identify the asset category.

2. Extract relevant financial features.

3. Recommend the optimal valuation methodology.

4. Optionally estimate the asset’s fair value.

# 3\. Fundamental Principle: No-Arbitrage

The entire valuation framework relies on the principle of No-Arbitrage.

Definition:

No investment strategy should generate a risk-free profit without requiring capital or exposure to risk.

Implications:

* Forward pricing

* Futures pricing

* Option pricing

* Swap pricing

All valuation models generated by the ML system should satisfy arbitrage constraints.

# 4\. Time Value of Money

Future cash flows must be discounted.

Discrete Compounding:

PV \= CF / (1+r)^t

Continuous Compounding:

PV \= CF × exp(-rt)

Where:

PV \= Present Value

CF \= Future Cash Flow

r \= Interest Rate

t \= Time

Applications:

* Bond valuation

* Swap valuation

* Option pricing

* Credit valuation

# 8\. Forward Contract Valuation

Forward contracts are OTC agreements to buy or sell an asset in the future at a predetermined price.

Theoretical Forward Price:

F \= S × exp(rT)

Where:

F \= Forward Price

S \= Spot Price

r \= Risk-Free Rate

T \= Time to Maturity

Applications:

* FX Forwards

* Commodity Forwards

* Equity Forwards

# 10\. Cost of Carry Model

Used extensively for commodities.

Formula:

F \= S × exp((r \+ u \- y)T)

Where:

r \= Interest Rate

u \= Storage Cost

y \= Convenience Yield

Applications:

* Gold

* Oil

* Agricultural Commodities

# 12\. Risk-Neutral Valuation

Modern derivatives pricing uses a risk-neutral probability measure.

General Formula:

Value \= Discounted Expected Payoff

Applications:

* Black-Scholes

* Binomial Trees

* Monte Carlo Simulation

# 13\. Binomial Tree Model

Suitable for:

* American Options

* Early Exercise Features

Steps:

1. Build price tree.

2. Compute terminal payoffs.

3. Perform backward induction.

4. Obtain present value.

Advantages:

* Intuitive

* Flexible

---

# 14\. Stochastic Processes

Asset prices are modeled as stochastic variables.

Geometric Brownian Motion:

dS \= μSdt \+ σSdW

Parameters:

μ \= Drift

σ \= Volatility

dW \= Brownian Motion

Applications:

* Option pricing

* Scenario generation

* Monte Carlo simulation

# 18\. Monte Carlo Simulation

Procedure:

1. Simulate thousands of future paths.

2. Calculate payoffs.

3. Compute average payoff.

4. Discount to present value.

Applications:

* Exotic Options

* Structured Products

* Portfolio Risk

Advantages:

* Extremely flexible

* Handles complex payoffs
